# Phase 0.4 — Protocol Stress Test

**Status:** Complete  
**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Outcome:** PASS WITH AMENDMENTS; all amendments incorporated into CAN-CCHD Protocol v1.0 before formal search execution.

## Purpose

The stress test was designed to challenge the draft protocol using heterogeneous sentinel studies before any formal search, screening, extraction, or meta-analysis under the new review framework.

The exercise was not used as quantitative extraction and no sentinel-study values are inherited as analytic data. Its only purpose was to identify ambiguous or unstable protocol rules.

## Sentinel-study stress domains

The test deliberately included studies representing:

- early historical terminology predating modern CCHD nomenclature;
- early versus later screening timing;
- single and repeated oximetry measurements;
- multicenter and population-based screening;
- different reporting styles for false positives and secondary conditions;
- incomplete follow-up of failed screens;
- non-critical congenital heart disease requiring follow-up;
- grouped actionability statements;
- transitional physiology;
- internally inconsistent screening-flow denominators.

Representative sentinel studies included Koppel et al., Sendelbach et al., de-Wahl Granelli et al., Ewer/PulseOx, POLAR, Maryland program data, Schwartz et al., Taipei program data, and a Moroccan screening cohort.

## Amendments generated by the stress test

### 1. Harmonized CCHD definition becomes primary

**Draft problem:** Using each study's own definition of CCHD as the primary denominator classification would make historical studies semantically non-comparable because terms such as `critical congenital cardiovascular malformation`, `duct-dependent congenital heart disease`, `major congenital heart defect`, and modern `CCHD` do not map perfectly.

**v1.0 rule:** The primary analytic denominator uses a harmonized review definition based on neonatal life-threatening/duct-dependent disease and neonatal invasive intervention logic, informed by the 2018 Cochrane framework. Study-defined terminology remains preserved as a secondary variable.

### 2. CAN-AB added

**Draft problem:** Some studies explicitly prove that a counted group required clinically meaningful intervention or follow-up but do not permit separation into acute treatment (CAN-A) versus management/follow-up (CAN-B).

**v1.0 rule:** Add `CAN-AB — Strict actionable, acuity subtype not separable`. CAN-AB enters the Strict CAN-CCHD numerator.

### 3. Unique newborn after final failure is the analytic unit

**Draft problem:** Studies may report large numbers of initially abnormal oximetry measurements that normalize after protocol-defined retesting.

**v1.0 rule:** Count the unique newborn once only after the study's final protocol-defined failure criterion is met. Initial abnormal measurements are not failed screens when the newborn later passes the algorithm.

### 4. Definitive actionability requires primary evidence

**Draft problem:** Secondary reviews may describe the clinical meaning of a false-positive case more completely than the abstract or primary report excerpt initially available.

**v1.0 rule:** Final actionability must derive from a primary report or complementary report from the same study cluster. Secondary literature is limited to citation chasing and discrepancy identification.

### 5. Transitional physiology rule added

**Draft problem:** Delayed transition/transient neonatal hypoxemia can be reported as a non-CCHD diagnosis but may resolve without any meaningful management consequence.

**v1.0 rule:** Transitional physiology is Non-CAN when it resolves without condition-specific treatment, disposition change, or follow-up. It becomes Strict CAN only when explicit qualifying clinical action is documented.

### 6. Denominator provenance/status formalized

**Draft problem:** Some reports contain disagreement between text, tables, flow diagrams, or follow-up counts.

**v1.0 rule:** Every analytic denominator is coded as `direct_reported`, `calculated_unambiguously`, `author_clarified`, or `unresolved`. Unresolved denominators cannot enter affected primary pooled estimates.

### 7. Group-level actionability allowed under strict conditions

**Draft problem:** Important historical studies may state that a defined group of non-CCHD illnesses required urgent intervention without giving management details for each individual diagnosis.

**v1.0 rule:** A counted group may enter Strict CAN-CCHD when the primary report explicitly links that same counted group to qualifying clinical action. Individual-level action details are not required if group linkage is unambiguous.

### 8. Generic Discussion statements cannot establish actionability

**Draft problem:** Statements that secondary diseases 'may benefit from early treatment' could be misread as evidence that the study's actual cases received meaningful management.

**v1.0 rule:** Generic discussion/rationale text does not establish actionability for study participants.

### 9. Screen-detected attribution requires explicit temporal evidence

**Draft problem:** An asymptomatic or routine-screening population does not prove that a condition was discovered because of the pulse-ox screen.

**v1.0 rule:** `screen-detected` requires evidence that the condition was not previously known, was not already under condition-specific investigation, and that the failed screen initiated or precipitated the diagnostic pathway. Otherwise use `screen-contributed`, `pre-known`, or `attribution unclear`.

### 10. >=90% outcome ascertainment retained for the primary CAN-CCHD analysis

**Stress-test finding:** Studies can report clinically meaningful non-CCHD outcomes while leaving a large proportion of CCHD-negative failed screens without documented final outcome.

**v1.0 rule:** Primary pooled CAN-CCHD estimates require at least 90% non-CCHD outcome ascertainment. Studies below this threshold remain eligible for the systematic review but are excluded from the affected primary pooled estimate.

## Conclusion

The stress test did not identify a fatal problem with the CAN-CCHD hypothesis or the Etiology × Actionability × Attribution framework. Instead, it identified correctable ambiguity in CCHD harmonization, actionability granularity, screening-unit definition, attribution, transitional physiology, and denominator integrity.

All changes were made before formal search execution and therefore do not represent outcome-driven post-hoc adaptation.

**Phase 0.4 result: PASS WITH AMENDMENTS.**
