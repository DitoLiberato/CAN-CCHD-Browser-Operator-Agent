# CAN-CCHD Systematic Review Protocol v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date locked:** 2026-08-20  
**Status:** Phase 0 complete; protocol frozen before formal search execution  
**Working title:** *Beyond False Positives: The Clinical Yield of Newborn Pulse Oximetry Screening for Critical Congenital Heart Disease — A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis*

---

## 1. Scientific rationale

Conventional evaluations of newborn pulse-oximetry screening for critical congenital heart disease (CCHD) emphasize CCHD-specific diagnostic accuracy and classify failed screens without CCHD as false positives. However, professional guidance and primary studies have repeatedly shown that a substantial subset of CCHD-negative failed screens identifies other hypoxemic or clinically important neonatal conditions.

This review treats a CCHD-specific false positive as a diagnostic-accuracy label, not necessarily as a clinically unproductive event. Its central hypothesis is that the conventional false-positive rate conflates two clinically different outcomes:

1. failed screens associated with a clinically actionable non-CCHD condition; and
2. failed screens without any clinically actionable condition.

The review introduces **CAN-CCHD (Clinically Actionable Non-CCHD Condition)** as an operational outcome framework intended to quantify the clinically productive component of CCHD-negative failed screens.

The review does **not** claim to measure the total health-system impact of CCHD screening, which would also require mortality, cost-effectiveness, resource use, parental burden, false-negative outcomes, and longer-term morbidity. The target construct is the **clinical yield of a failed screening episode**.

---

## 2. Primary review question

> Among newborns who have a final failed routine pulse-oximetry screen for CCHD and are subsequently classified as not having harmonized CCHD, what proportion have a clinically actionable non-CCHD condition?

---

## 3. Primary objective

Estimate the proportion of harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens associated with **Strict CAN-CCHD**.

\[
Strict\ CAN\text{-}CCHD\ Yield =
\frac{N_{CAN-A}+N_{CAN-B}+N_{CAN-AB}}
{N_{harmonized\ CCHD-negative\ final\ failed\ screens}}
\]

---

## 4. Secondary objectives

Estimate, where data permit:

1. Expanded CAN-CCHD yield including CAN-U.
2. Overall actionable yield among all final failed screens.
3. Harmonized CCHD yield among final failed screens.
4. Non-actionable failed-screen proportion among all final failed screens.
5. Strict and Expanded CAN-CCHD cases per 10,000 newborns screened.
6. Non-actionable failed screens per 10,000 newborns screened.
7. Etiologic composition of non-CCHD conditions among failed screens.
8. Acute versus management/follow-up actionability.
9. Screen-detected versus screen-associated CAN-CCHD.
10. Downstream diagnostic and resource consequences of failed screens, reported separately from clinical yield.
11. Availability and completeness of non-CCHD outcome reporting across otherwise eligible CCHD-screening studies.

---

## 5. Conceptual model

```text
ALL NEWBORNS SCREENED
        |
        +-- PASS
        |
        +-- FINAL FAILED SCREEN
                 |
                 +-- Harmonized CCHD
                 |
                 +-- Harmonized CCHD-negative
                         |
                         +-- CAN-A
                         +-- CAN-B
                         +-- CAN-AB
                         +-- CAN-U
                         +-- Non-CAN
                         +-- Indeterminate / outcome unknown
```

Each non-CCHD condition will be characterized across three independent dimensions:

```text
Etiology × Actionability × Attribution
```

---

## 6. Definition of CAN-CCHD

> **Clinically Actionable Non-CCHD Condition (CAN-CCHD):** a non-CCHD condition identified in association with a final failed newborn pulse-oximetry screening episode for which the available study evidence demonstrates a condition-specific change in clinical management, disposition, treatment, or follow-up.

The performance of an investigation solely because the screening algorithm mandates that investigation does **not** establish actionability.

Examples that do not by themselves establish CAN-CCHD include echocardiography, blood tests, cultures, chest radiography, observation, or specialist review performed solely because a failed-screen protocol required them.

---

## 7. Actionability framework

### CAN-A — Acute actionable

A non-CCHD condition associated with acute treatment or escalation of care.

Examples include, when explicitly linked to the condition:

- antimicrobial treatment;
- oxygen therapy;
- CPAP or mechanical ventilation;
- treatment directed at persistent pulmonary hypertension;
- NICU transfer or higher-acuity transfer;
- urgent procedure or other acute therapy;
- active warming for clinically significant hypothermia.

### CAN-B — Management actionable

A non-CCHD condition that changes disposition, follow-up, surveillance, or management without documented acute therapeutic escalation.

Examples include:

- disease-specific prolongation of hospitalization;
- cardiology follow-up for non-critical CHD;
- hematology or other specialist follow-up;
- disease-specific planned investigation or surveillance not otherwise required by the screening algorithm.

### CAN-AB — Strict actionable; acuity subtype not separable

The study explicitly establishes that the counted group required clinically meaningful treatment, intervention, referral, or follow-up, but the report does not allow reliable separation into CAN-A versus CAN-B.

CAN-AB is part of **Strict CAN-CCHD**.

### CAN-U — Actionability unconfirmed

A potentially clinically important non-CCHD diagnosis is reported, but the report does not provide sufficient evidence that the diagnosis produced a condition-specific change in management, disposition, treatment, or follow-up.

CAN-U is excluded from the primary Strict CAN-CCHD numerator and included only in Expanded CAN-CCHD sensitivity analyses.

### Non-CAN

No clinically actionable disease is demonstrated.

Examples include:

- no condition identified;
- incidental physiologic findings without disease-specific management;
- clinically insignificant PFO or small physiologic PDA without disease-specific follow-up;
- transient hypoxemia/delayed transition resolving without condition-specific treatment, disposition change, or follow-up.

### Indeterminate

The report does not provide sufficient information to classify the non-CCHD state or its actionability.

No value will be imputed.

---

## 8. Evidence hierarchy for actionability

Actionability will be coded only from primary reports or complementary reports belonging to the same study cluster.

### Level 1 — Individual explicit evidence

The report links a named condition in an individual newborn to treatment, escalation, disposition change, or follow-up.

### Level 2 — Group explicit evidence

The report explicitly states that a counted group of non-CCHD conditions required treatment, urgent intervention, referral, hospitalization, follow-up, or another qualifying management action.

Individual-level management details are not required if the counted group is unambiguous.

### Level 3 — Diagnosis group plus explicit management statement

The diagnostic group and a linked management consequence are explicit, but CAN-A versus CAN-B cannot be separated. Such cases may be coded CAN-AB.

### Level 4 — Diagnosis only

The report identifies a relevant diagnosis but does not establish a management consequence. Code CAN-U unless other study-cluster reports resolve actionability.

### Level 5 — Generic discussion statement

General statements such as “these diseases may benefit from early treatment” do not establish actionability in the study population and cannot contribute to the Strict CAN-CCHD numerator.

Secondary reviews may be used for citation chasing or discrepancy detection but cannot establish final actionability when primary reports are available.

---

## 9. Etiologic framework

The original terminology used by each study will be preserved verbatim. Where possible, diagnoses will additionally be mapped to the broad non-CCHD categories used in current AAP guidance:

- persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN);
- infection, including sepsis;
- lung disease, congenital or acquired;
- non-critical congenital heart defect;
- hemoglobinopathy;
- hypothermia;
- other hypoxemic condition not otherwise specified;
- other actionable non-CCHD condition;
- unclear.

Etiologic categories are **not assumed to be mutually exclusive**.

Counts from etiologic categories must never be summed to reconstruct `any CAN-CCHD` unless the primary study explicitly establishes that the categories represent unique, mutually exclusive newborns or individual-level data permit unique-person reconstruction.

---

## 10. Attribution framework

Actionability and causal attribution are separate constructs.

### Screen-detected

All of the following are supported by the report:

1. the condition was not previously known;
2. condition-specific investigation was not already underway before the failed screen; and
3. the failed screen initiated or precipitated the investigation leading to diagnosis.

### Screen-contributed

There were concurrent clinical findings or suspicion, but the failed screen materially accelerated or contributed to investigation or management.

### Pre-known

The relevant condition was diagnosed before the screening episode.

### Attribution unclear

The timing and diagnostic pathway cannot be reconstructed reliably.

The primary CAN-CCHD analysis is **screen-associated** and does not require causal attribution. Screen-detected CAN-CCHD will be reported as a stricter secondary/sensitivity analysis when data permit.

No study will be described as demonstrating screening-attributable benefit unless the attribution criteria are met.

---

## 11. Population

### Primary population

Newborns undergoing **routine pulse-oximetry screening** intended to detect CCHD or historical equivalents in settings such as:

- well-baby nursery;
- maternity/newborn ward;
- birth hospital;
- routine out-of-hospital/home-birth program;
- regional or population-wide newborn screening program.

The screening test must be used as a screening intervention rather than a diagnostic test ordered because the newborn is already clinically ill.

### NICU-only populations

NICU-only cohorts are excluded from the primary pool because baseline hypoxemia, supplemental oxygen exposure, prematurity, and pre-existing disease materially alter the meaning of a failed screening result.

NICU-only studies may be retained for a separate population analysis if sufficient evidence accumulates.

### Mixed populations

Studies combining routine well-baby screening and NICU/high-risk populations may enter the primary pool only if the routine-screening population can be separated or if the study represents universal routine screening with sufficient population characterization for a prespecified mixed-population subgroup.

---

## 12. Definition of the screening episode and final failed screen

> A **final failed screen** is a screening episode that meets the study protocol’s final criteria for failure after completion of all protocol-defined repeat measurements.

The unit of analysis is the **unique newborn**, not an oximetry measurement.

Therefore:

```text
initial abnormal measurement -> protocol-defined repeat -> final pass
```

is **not** a final failed screen.

A newborn is counted once as a failed screen even if multiple abnormal measurements occurred.

Studies reporting only abnormal initial measurements without permitting identification of final failed screening episodes cannot contribute to analyses requiring a final-failed denominator.

---

## 13. Harmonized definition of CCHD

### Primary classification

The primary denominator will use a **harmonized review definition of CCHD**, rather than each study’s historical label alone.

The harmonized definition follows the principle used in the 2018 Cochrane diagnostic-accuracy review: a potentially life-threatening duct-dependent congenital heart lesion associated with death or requirement for invasive cardiac intervention during the neonatal period, operationalized where possible around the first 28 days of life.

Lesions explicitly encompassed by the harmonized framework include classic critical lesions such as:

- hypoplastic left heart syndrome;
- pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum;
- simple transposition of the great arteries;
- interrupted aortic arch;

and severity-dependent lesions such as coarctation of the aorta, critical aortic or pulmonary stenosis, tetralogy of Fallot, pulmonary atresia with VSD, and total anomalous pulmonary venous connection when neonatal death or invasive intervention criteria are met.

Current AAP/CDC core-condition lists will be used as a contemporary cross-reference, while preserving the intervention/severity logic required for historical cohorts.

### Harmonization confidence

Each study/lesion classification will be coded:

- **Exact:** lesion and clinical course permit direct harmonized classification;
- **Compatible:** the study’s target definition is sufficiently congruent with the harmonized review definition to classify aggregate counts reliably;
- **Uncertain:** available information is insufficient to classify the study’s CCHD counts reliably.

Primary CAN-CCHD analyses may include Exact and Compatible classifications. Uncertain classifications are excluded from primary estimates that depend on the harmonized CCHD-negative denominator and may be included in sensitivity or descriptive analyses.

### Historical definition preservation

The following will also be extracted:

- `study_target_definition_verbatim`;
- `study_defined_CCHD_count`;
- `harmonized_CCHD_count`;
- `harmonization_confidence`;
- rationale/source for any difference.

No historical patient or study will be silently reclassified.

---

## 14. Inclusion criteria for the systematic review evidence base

A study is eligible for the systematic review evidence base if it:

1. includes newborn/neonatal screening populations;
2. evaluates pulse oximetry used as routine CCHD screening or a historical equivalent;
3. permits identification of final failed screens, or provides data relevant to the characterization of failed-screen outcomes;
4. reports or can contribute information about outcomes following failed screens.

Eligible designs include:

- prospective screening cohorts;
- retrospective cohorts;
- hospital, regional, population, or national screening programs;
- implementation studies;
- surveillance datasets;
- diagnostic-accuracy cohorts;
- randomized or quasi-randomized studies when the screening arm provides eligible data;
- consecutive cohorts specifically of failed-screen newborns when a valid denominator is available.

There is no arbitrary minimum sample size.

A study may remain in the systematic review even when CAN-CCHD outcomes are not reported sufficiently for quantitative pooling. Such studies will be coded as eligible screening studies with unavailable or incomplete CAN outcome reporting.

This distinction is intended to reduce selective-outcome availability bias.

---

## 15. Exclusion criteria

Exclude from the primary systematic review evidence base:

- non-neonatal populations;
- non-human studies;
- pulse oximetry used primarily for diagnostic evaluation of an already symptomatic/ill infant rather than routine screening;
- studies unrelated to CCHD screening;
- device/engineering validation without clinical screening outcomes;
- case reports;
- non-consecutive case series without a valid denominator;
- editorials, commentaries, and letters without original eligible data;
- narrative or systematic reviews as primary data sources;
- duplicate reports treated as independent studies.

NICU-only studies are excluded from the primary population synthesis but may be retained for separate analysis.

No study will be excluded solely because of language, country, direction of result, statistical significance, absence of CAN-CCHD, or publication status when verifiable data are available.

---

## 16. Study-level versus outcome-level eligibility

Eligibility for the review and eligibility for a given meta-analysis are distinct.

For the **primary Strict CAN-CCHD meta-analysis**, the study must provide or permit reliable derivation of:

- unique newborns with final failed screens;
- harmonized CCHD count or a Compatible aggregate equivalent;
- harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens;
- unique newborns meeting Strict CAN-CCHD criteria;
- outcome ascertainment sufficient for the primary analysis.

A study that reports individual etiologic counts but does not permit unique-person reconstruction may contribute to diagnosis-specific analyses but not to `any CAN-CCHD`.

---

## 17. Outcome ascertainment

For each study:

\[
Outcome\ ascertainment =
\frac{CCHD-negative\ failed\ screens\ with\ known\ non-CCHD\ outcome}
{all\ CCHD-negative\ final\ failed\ screens}
\]

Classification:

- **Complete:** 100%
- **Near-complete:** 90–99%
- **Incomplete:** <90%
- **Unclear:** denominator or outcome coverage cannot be reconstructed.

The primary pooled CAN-CCHD analysis requires at least **90% outcome ascertainment**.

For studies with 90–99% ascertainment, all eligible CCHD-negative failed screens remain in the primary denominator and unknown outcomes are not counted as CAN-CCHD, producing a conservative estimate.

Sensitivity analyses will include:

- 100% ascertainment only;
- complete-case denominators;
- plausible best/worst bounds when missingness is material and interpretable.

Missing outcome data will never be automatically treated as zero CAN-CCHD at the individual level.

---

## 18. Denominator integrity and inconsistent study flow

Every analytic denominator will be coded:

- **direct_reported**;
- **calculated_unambiguously**;
- **author_clarified**;
- **unresolved**.

When text, tables, figures, flow diagrams, supplements, or companion reports disagree, reconciliation will proceed in the following order:

1. primary full text;
2. supplementary material;
3. companion reports from the same study cluster;
4. correction/erratum;
5. author contact where feasible.

If an outcome-relevant denominator remains unresolved, the study is excluded from the primary pooled estimate requiring that denominator but may remain in the systematic review and other analyses not dependent on the unresolved value.

---

## 19. Transitional physiology rule

Delayed transition, transient neonatal hypoxemia, or transitional circulation is **not automatically CAN-CCHD**.

Classify as Non-CAN when it resolves without condition-specific treatment, disposition change, or follow-up.

Classify as Strict CAN-CCHD only when persistent/clinically significant hypoxemia produces a qualifying treatment, escalation of care, disease-specific prolonged observation/admission, or follow-up.

When the article reports only a transitional diagnosis without management consequences, classify as CAN-U or Indeterminate as appropriate.

---

## 20. Non-critical congenital heart disease rule

Non-critical CHD is not automatically CAN-CCHD.

- acute treatment/intervention -> CAN-A;
- disease-specific cardiology follow-up or surveillance -> CAN-B;
- explicit actionable group but A/B not separable -> CAN-AB;
- diagnosis reported without management consequence -> CAN-U;
- physiologic/incidental finding without disease-specific management -> Non-CAN.

---

## 21. Diagnostic burden versus productive clinical yield

The review will distinguish:

### Productive downstream action

A failed screen leading to identification of a Strict or Expanded CAN-CCHD condition.

### Non-productive diagnostic cascade

Investigations or monitoring triggered by a failed screen without identification of an actionable condition.

Potential resource-use variables include:

- echocardiography;
- laboratory testing/cultures;
- imaging;
- observation;
- prolonged hospitalization;
- NICU transfer;
- specialist review.

These outcomes will be reported separately. No arbitrary “net benefit score” will be created.

---

## 22. Study as the unit of interest

The unit of evidence is the **study/cohort**, not the publication.

Multiple articles, abstracts, surveillance reports, or follow-up reports arising from the same underlying population will be linked under a `study_cluster_id`.

Secondary reports will not be discarded because they may provide complementary algorithm, denominator, follow-up, or actionability data.

---

## 23. Overlapping cohorts

For each outcome, the preferred source/report will be selected according to:

1. clearest failed-screen outcome characterization;
2. most reliable denominator;
3. most complete outcome ascertainment;
4. largest non-overlapping population;
5. full peer-reviewed report where otherwise comparable;
6. most current corrected report.

Information may be combined across reports within one study cluster without treating reports as independent studies.

If partially overlapping populations cannot be separated reliably, only one overlapping dataset will contribute to a given pooled outcome.

A cohort-overlap matrix will be maintained.

## 25. Risk of bias and outcome confidence

### Primary risk-of-bias framework

The JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Prevalence Studies will be used as the principal structured appraisal because the primary meta-analytic estimand is a study-level proportion.

Items will be recorded as `Yes / No / Unclear / Not applicable`. No summed numeric “quality score” will be used to determine inclusion.

Design-specific supplementary appraisal may be added when an unusual design creates risks not captured by the prevalence framework.

### CAN outcome ascertainment confidence

Separately from formal risk of bias, the review will code a review-specific outcome-confidence descriptor:

**High**
- outcome accounting complete or near-complete;
- diagnoses clearly described;
- management explicitly linked;
- unique newborns identifiable.

**Moderate**
- outcome accounting adequate;
- some grouped management or limited ambiguity remains.

**Low**
- substantial uncertainty in outcome coverage, overlap, or management characterization.

This is not presented as a validated risk-of-bias instrument and will not be converted into a numeric score.

## 26. Primary statistical model

The principal synthesis is a meta-analysis of single proportions.

Where at least three clinically sufficiently comparable studies are available, the primary model will be a **random-effects binomial generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) with logit link**.

The model will use study-level event counts and denominators directly and will not rely on arbitrary continuity corrections for zero-event studies.

Pooling will not be performed solely because `k >= 3`; clinical and methodological comparability remains required.

For each pooled estimate, report as appropriate:

- pooled proportion;
- 95% confidence interval;
- between-study variance (tau-squared or model-equivalent heterogeneity parameter);
- heterogeneity description;
- forest plot;
- 95% prediction interval when the number and distribution of studies make it interpretable.

I-squared may be reported descriptively but will not alone determine whether pooling is appropriate.

Freeman-Tukey double-arcsine transformation will not be the primary analytic method.

## 27. Prespecified quantitative outcomes

### Primary

**Strict CAN-CCHD yield among harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens**

\[
\frac{CAN-A + CAN-B + CAN-AB}{harmonized\ CCHD-negative\ final\ failed\ screens}
\]

### Key secondary

**Expanded CAN-CCHD yield**

\[
\frac{CAN-A + CAN-B + CAN-AB + CAN-U}{harmonized\ CCHD-negative\ final\ failed\ screens}
\]

**Overall actionable yield among all final failed screens**

\[
\frac{harmonized\ CCHD + Strict\ CAN-CCHD}{all\ final\ failed\ screens}
\]

**Non-actionable failed-screen proportion**

\[
\frac{final\ failed\ screens\ without\ actionable\ disease}{all\ final\ failed\ screens}
\]

### Population outcomes

Where `N_screened` is reliable, report per 10,000 screened:

- harmonized CCHD;
- Strict CAN-CCHD;
- Expanded CAN-CCHD;
- actionable failed screens;
- non-actionable failed screens.

### Etiologic analyses

Potential pooled proportions among CCHD-negative failed screens include:

- PPHN;
- infection/sepsis;
- lung disease;
- non-critical CHD;
- hypothermia;
- hemoglobinopathy;
- other hypoxemic conditions;
- other actionable conditions.

Etiologic proportions must never be summed to derive `any CAN-CCHD` unless unique-person counts are established.

Priority subgroup analyses:

### Screening timing

- <24 hours;
- >=24 hours;
- mixed/unclear.

### Screening algorithm

- preductal + postductal;
- postductal only;
- other.

Thresholds and repeat rules will also be extracted.

### Population

- well-baby/asymptomatic routine screening;
- universal/mixed routine cohort.

### Prenatal/previous diagnosis handling

- known CCHD excluded before screening;
- known CCHD included;
- unclear.

### CAN outcome ascertainment method

- systematic explicit characterization of all/near-all failed screens;
- secondary conditions reported incompletely or incidentally.

## 29. Meta-regression

Meta-regression will be considered only if the evidence base is sufficiently large and covariates have adequate distribution.

Priority covariates:

1. screening timing;
2. screening algorithm;
3. prenatal diagnosis handling.

As an operational safeguard, approximately 10 studies per modeled covariate will be sought before interpretive meta-regression. Multivariable models will be avoided unless the dataset clearly supports them.

---

## 30. Prespecified sensitivity analyses

1. Strict versus Expanded CAN-CCHD.
2. 100% outcome ascertainment only.
3. Well-baby/asymptomatic populations only.
4. Prospective studies only.
5. Peer-reviewed full-text reports only.
6. Excluding studies with serious risk-of-bias concerns.
7. Exact harmonized-CCHD classification only versus Exact + Compatible.
8. Screen-detected CAN-CCHD only.
9. Screening at >=24 hours only.
10. Leave-one-study-out analysis when pooling size permits useful interpretation.
11. Alternative GLMM link or model specification for very rare population-level outcomes if prespecified diagnostics indicate the primary model is unstable.

---

## 31. Missing data

The review will never assume:

```text
missing = zero
```

or

```text
diagnosis mentioned = actionable
```

Resolution sequence:

1. examine all reports in the study cluster;
2. derive values only when algebraically unambiguous;
3. seek author clarification when feasible and potentially outcome-changing;
4. retain as missing/unclear if unresolved.

All derived values will retain provenance.

---

## 32. Search and study-selection safeguards

Formal database searches begin only after this protocol is locked.

Search strategies must:

- prioritize sensitivity over precision;
- use controlled vocabulary plus free-text synonyms where supported;
- avoid requiring modern `CCHD` terminology as the only heart-disease concept;
- be tested against a prespecified landmark/sentinel paper set before approval;
- undergo PRESS-style peer review when feasible by an information specialist or experienced medical librarian;
- preserve exact query strings, date/time, platform, result count, filters, and raw exports.

Title/abstract screening will be deliberately over-inclusive. Duplicate independent title/abstract screening is desirable; single-human initial screening is acceptable if required by practical constraints.

Final full-text eligibility decisions must be made independently by **two human reviewers**, with disagreements resolved by consensus and, if necessary, adjudication.

AI may prioritize, suggest, organize, and flag records but is not counted as an independent human reviewer and cannot make final inclusion decisions.

---

## 33. Data extraction and verification safeguards

Outcome data feeding meta-analysis must be extracted independently by at least **two human reviewers**, or independently extracted by one reviewer and fully independently re-extracted/verified by a second reviewer according to a predefined reconciliation process.

At minimum, the following require dual human verification:

- final failed-screen denominator;
- harmonized CCHD classification/count;
- harmonized-CCHD-negative denominator;
- Strict/Expanded CAN-CCHD numerators;
- actionability class;
- attribution class when used analytically;
- overlap/unique-person status;
- study-cluster overlap decisions.

AI-generated extraction remains `AI-suggested` until human validation and cannot directly enter quantitative analysis.

Disagreements and final consensus values will be auditable.

---

## 34. Publication and outcome-reporting bias

The review will explicitly characterize the availability of non-CCHD outcome data across all otherwise eligible CCHD-screening studies.

Eligible studies will be categorized as:

- CAN outcome adequately reported;
- CAN outcome partially reported;
- CAN outcome not reported;
- outcome ascertainment unclear.

Characteristics of CAN-reporting versus non-reporting studies will be compared descriptively to evaluate selective outcome availability.

Traditional funnel plots or small-study-effect tests will be used cautiously and only when the number and distribution of studies make them interpretable.

---

## 35. Grey literature and conference abstracts

Conference abstracts and other non-peer-reviewed reports may contribute when they contain verifiable eligible denominators and outcomes.

Primary estimates may include them when data quality is sufficient, with prespecified sensitivity analyses excluding non-peer-reviewed reports.

When an abstract and later full report represent the same cohort, they are one study cluster.

## 37. Phase 0.4 stress-test amendments incorporated into v1.0

The following amendments were adopted before protocol lock:

1. Harmonized CCHD became the primary CCHD classification for analytic denominators; historical study-defined CCHD is preserved as a secondary variable.
2. Added `CAN-AB` for explicit strict actionability when CAN-A versus CAN-B cannot be separated.
3. Clarified that the analytic unit is the unique newborn after protocol-defined final failure, not an abnormal measurement.
4. Required primary or same-study-cluster evidence for definitive actionability classification.
5. Added an explicit transitional-physiology rule.
6. Added denominator provenance/status and a rule excluding unresolved denominators from affected primary pooled outcomes.
7. Permitted group-level Strict CAN-CCHD classification only when the report explicitly links the counted group to qualifying management consequences.
8. Clarified that generic Discussion statements do not establish actionability in the study population.
9. Required explicit temporal evidence for `screen-detected` attribution.
10. Retained the >=90% non-CCHD outcome-ascertainment requirement for the primary CAN-CCHD analysis.

These changes arose from protocol stress testing against heterogeneous sentinel studies and were incorporated before the formal search and quantitative extraction phases.
