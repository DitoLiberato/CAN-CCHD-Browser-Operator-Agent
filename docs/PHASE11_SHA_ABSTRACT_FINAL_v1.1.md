## Title

**Beyond Target CCHD: Clinically Relevant Diagnoses and Outcomes After Failed Newborn Pulse Oximetry Screening—A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis**

## Abstract

### Introduction

False-positive newborn pulse oximetry screens for critical congenital heart disease (CCHD) may identify clinically important non-target conditions, but their frequency and consequences are uncertain.

### Methodology

We systematically reviewed reports of infants with final failed newborn pulse oximetry screens in whom target CCHD was not identified. Searches of PubMed/MEDLINE and complementary public-web sources (Europe PMC, LILACS/BVS, SciELO, and IMEMR/EMR), supplemented by grey literature and citation chasing, were completed on August 21, 2026. The primary denominator was harmonized CCHD-negative final failed screens. Strict clinically actionable non-CCHD (CAN-CCHD) required a prespecified diagnosis plus a qualifying management consequence; Expanded CAN-CCHD additionally included clinically relevant diagnoses without documented qualifying management. Prespecified denominator, ≥90% outcome-ascertainment, and primary-source verification rules were audited. Proportions were pooled using exact-binomial logistic-normal models.

### Results

Twenty-eight non-overlapping primary analytic units included 1,999 infants. Strict CAN-CCHD had a median-unit probability of 17.0% (95% CI, 3.1–46.8%), marginal mean 33.8%, and extreme heterogeneity (tau=3.369; 95% prediction interval, 0.03–99.3%). Expanded CAN-CCHD was more common but remained heterogeneous: median-unit probability 69.4% (57.7–81.4%), marginal mean 65.8%, tau=1.110 (prediction interval, 20.4–95.2%). In non-mutually-exclusive outcome-specific subsets (k=22–26), median-unit probabilities were 26.6% (14.4–43.0%) for other structural cardiac disease, 16.7% (9.4–24.2%) for infection, 10.3% (4.7–16.3%) for persistent pulmonary hypertension, and 8.7% (1.6–23.0%) for respiratory disease. Prespecified sensitivity analyses did not reverse the interpretation.

### Conclusion

Among final failed screens without target CCHD, clinically relevant alternative diagnoses or outcomes were common, whereas documentation of qualifying management consequences varied markedly across programs. For pediatric cardiologists involved in early evaluation after a failed screen, assessment may extend beyond excluding target CCHD to recognizing alternative neonatal conditions and facilitating multidisciplinary care.
