# CAN-CCHD Phase 11 — Pediatric Cardiology Positioning / Citation Mining

Date: 2026-08-22  
Branch: `phase11-sha-abstract`  
Status: **editorial_support_only — no Phase 6 scientific value changed**

## Question

Can the SHA abstract/discussion defensibly emphasize that pediatric cardiologists often enter the care pathway early after a failed newborn CCHD pulse-oximetry screen, so the encounter should be framed not only as exclusion of target CCHD but also as an opportunity to recognize other clinically important neonatal disease?

## Bottom line

**Yes, with careful wording.**

The literature strongly supports that failed CCHD screens require evaluation for causes of hypoxemia, often including echocardiography and pediatric-cardiology involvement. Multiple implemented screening programs explicitly refer failed infants to pediatric cardiologists, including immediate assessment by a senior pediatric cardiologist in some cohorts. However, the broader claim that a pediatric cardiologist is *frequently one of the first senior pediatricians to see the infant* is not sufficiently universal to state as an established cross-system fact.

Preferred framing:

> Pediatric cardiologists are frequent early recipients of escalation after a failed CCHD screen and may therefore encounter clinically important non-CCHD disease during the diagnostic pathway.

Avoid:

> Pediatric cardiologists are usually the first senior pediatricians to assess these infants.

The second formulation is highly pathway-dependent and exceeds the evidence.

## High-value sources

### 1. AAP 2025 clinical report

Oster ME, Pinto NM, Pramanik AK, et al. *Newborn Screening for Critical Congenital Heart Disease: A New Algorithm and Other Updated Recommendations: Clinical Report.* Pediatrics. 2025;155(1):e2024069667. DOI: 10.1542/peds.2024-069667. PMID: 39679594.

Key relevance:

- explicitly recommends educating stakeholders about the **significance of non-CCHD conditions**;
- explains that reducing retesting may slightly increase false positives, but this is balanced by the possibility of identifying **other clinically important disease**;
- recommends collecting the presence/type of non-CCHD conditions identified after failed screens.

This is the strongest external support for the CAN-CCHD conceptual frame.

### 2. CDC current clinical guidance

Current CDC clinician guidance based on the updated algorithm states that any infant who fails should be evaluated for causes of hypoxemia; in most cases this includes echocardiography, the pediatrician should be notified immediately, and cardiology assessment may be required.

Relevance:

- supports the failed screen as a **clinical escalation event**, not simply a test result;
- supports cardiology as part of the downstream diagnostic pathway without claiming universal first-line ownership.

### 3. Canadian Paediatric Society / Canadian cardiovascular-pediatric cardiology guidance

The Canadian guidance recommends thorough assessment after a failed screen and states that, when the cause appears cardiac or remains unclear, pediatric-cardiology consultation followed by echocardiography is required.

Relevance:

- demonstrates that cardiology referral after unresolved failed screening is embedded in formal clinical pathways;
- shows that the initial diagnostic responsibility may reside with another responsible provider, reinforcing the need to avoid claiming cardiologists are universally the first senior clinicians involved.

### 4. Saudi national screening experience

Alsaied T, et al. *Universal Screening for CCHD in Saudi Arabia: The Road to a 'State of the Art' Program.* Int J Neonatal Screen. 2020;6(1):13.

Key relevance:

- Saudi national recommendations state that newborns with positive results should receive thorough evaluation by the responsible provider and that pediatric-cardiology referral should be arranged when needed;
- if pediatric cardiology is not locally available, urgent referral to an appropriate center is recommended;
- this makes the positioning especially relevant to a Saudi Heart Association audience.

### 5. Saudi prospective screening cohort

Almawazini AM. *Effectiveness of the critical congenital heart disease screening program for early diagnosis of cardiac abnormalities in newborn infants.* Saudi Med J. 2017;38(10):1019-1024. DOI: 10.15537/smj.2017.10.20295.

Key relevance:

- echocardiography was performed by a pediatric cardiologist for all positive screens;
- pulse oximetry also identified life-threatening non-cardiac disorders including sepsis, pneumonia and pulmonary hypertension;
- authors emphasize collaboration between neonatologists and pediatric cardiologists.

This source provides unusually direct Saudi support for the proposed clinical framing.

### 6. Morocco implementation study

A failed screen was followed by pediatric-cardiologist examination and urgent cardiac ultrasound.

Relevance:

- direct example of cardiology being an early post-failure diagnostic contact.

### 7. Jordan prospective cohort

After failed screening, infants were referred to a **senior pediatric cardiologist for immediate assessment** and echocardiography.

Relevance:

- directly supports the existence of pathways in which a senior pediatric cardiologist is among the earliest senior physicians to evaluate the infant;
- should be used as an example, not generalized as a universal model.

## Relation to frozen CAN-CCHD findings

Frozen Phase 6 results show that, among 28 independent primary units and 1,999 harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens:

- Strict CAN-CCHD median-study probability = 17.0% (95% CI 3.1%-46.8%), with extreme heterogeneity;
- Expanded CAN-CCHD median-study probability = 69.4% (57.7%-81.4%);
- secondary etiologic estimates include other/non-target structural cardiac diagnoses 26.6%, infection/sepsis 16.7%, PPHN/PH 10.3%, and respiratory disease 8.7%.

The external literature does **not** change these results. It helps interpret the clinical meaning of the post-failure encounter.

## Recommended abstract-level positioning

The safest and strongest conference-level implication is not that cardiologists 'own' the failed screen, but that the failed-screen pathway places pediatric cardiologists in a strategically important diagnostic position.

Candidate implication concept:

> Because failed CCHD screens commonly trigger pediatric or pediatric-cardiology evaluation, these findings broaden the clinical meaning of that encounter: excluding target CCHD may be only one part of the diagnostic task.

More SHA-specific but still defensible:

> For pediatric cardiologists, a failed CCHD screen should be viewed not only as a referral to exclude target heart disease, but also as a marker of substantial risk for clinically relevant alternative neonatal disease.

The second sentence is suitable as a possible conclusion/discussion direction, but should not imply that cardiology alone is responsible for diagnosing or managing infection, respiratory disease or other systemic conditions.

## Recommendation for v0.3

Use this positioning as a **clinical implication sentence**, probably in the Conclusion, rather than spending abstract words on claims about referral hierarchy. The abstract itself likely does not need citations if the SHA form does not request references; the citation support should be preserved for the manuscript Discussion and for oral-presentation framing.
