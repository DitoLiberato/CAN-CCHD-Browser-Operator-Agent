# Case Report 01 — Phase 1 Scientific Saturation Audit v1.0

**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Branch:** `case-report-01`  
**Status:** **DISCOVERY / CITATION SATURATION = PASS**  
**Formal Gate 1:** **TECHNICAL REPRODUCIBILITY SUBGATE REMAINS OPEN**

## 1. Purpose

This audit answers a narrow question:

> Has continued searching across meaningfully different discovery pathways stopped yielding new individually identifiable direct TOF–Morgagni patients or new series signals that could materially expand the direct corpus?

This is distinct from the administrative/reproducibility requirement to preserve exact source-native query counts and exports for PubMed and Europe PMC.

## 2. Saturation criterion

Scientific saturation is accepted when:

1. the known modern direct sentinel cases are recovered;
2. historical terminology variants have been searched;
3. broader congenital diaphragmatic hernia searches have been anatomically adjudicated for noise;
4. Pentalogy/Cantrell pathways have been searched;
5. hidden cases inside Morgagni series have been actively sought;
6. adult/repaired-TOF presentations have been sought;
7. recent and grey/conference literature has been sought;
8. multilingual modern-term searches have been performed;
9. backward and forward citation chasing has been performed on direct candidates;
10. at least three consecutive conceptually independent late waves yield no new individually extractable direct patient after reconciliation.

## 3. Yield by discovery wave

### Wave A — modern exact TOF + Morgagni terminology

Recovered the known modern PubMed-indexed sentinel reports and additional direct web/journal cases.

Result: **positive yield; not saturated.**

### Wave B — historical/anatomic terminology

Expansion to `subcostosternal`, `subcosto-sternal`, `Larrey`, `retrosternal`, `parasternal` and anterior diaphragmatic terminology recovered the 1952 Johnson & Mangiardi report explicitly describing TOF with a subcostosternal defect.

Result: **new direct candidate; historical terminology essential.**

### Wave C — broad TOF + congenital diaphragmatic hernia sensitivity

Recovered both useful broader analogs and anatomically non-Morgagni cases. Hatherley 1950 was confirmed posterolateral and therefore excluded from the direct set.

Result: **no additional direct Morgagni patient after anatomy adjudication; useful noise control.**

### Wave D — Pentalogy of Cantrell / anterior midline sensitivity

Recovered a 2024 conference abstract with operative Morgagni repair and postoperative TOF diagnosis, plus a 2025/2026 TOF + large CDH case using RVOT stenting before definitive combined repair whose exact diaphragmatic anatomy remains unresolved from the accessible abstract.

Result: **one new direct candidate + one high-value anatomy-pending management analog.**

### Wave E — hidden-case mining inside Morgagni case series

This was the most productive late wave.

It recovered:

- Sönmez et al. 2006: a tabled 12-month-old patient with Morgagni hernia, cyanosis and associated Tetralogy of Fallot — individually extractable direct case;
- Ortiz et al. 2025: 55 children with Morgagni hernia, including **two** associated TOF cases, but without sufficient patient-level mapping in the accessible report for individual extraction;
- Al-Salem multicenter literature: aggregate congenital-heart-disease signal with an external/secondary TOF signal, but no currently defensible patient-level TOF count from the accessible primary abstract;
- Tan tertiary-center experience: one incidental Morgagni repaired during cardiac surgery, but the cardiac diagnosis is not identified in accessible material;
- Bawazir series: frequent post-cardiac-correction Morgagni/anterior defects but no TOF signal identified in the accessible full article;
- a Columbia CDH/CHD cohort in which the Morgagni subgroup's identified cardiac lesions were ASD/VSD rather than TOF.

Result: **one new individually extractable direct case + one two-patient aggregate TOF signal + unresolved/negative series controls.**

### Wave F — adult/repaired-TOF searches

Recovered Rao et al. 2014: repaired TOF with a large Morgagni hernia and a detailed competing-physiology assessment in adulthood.

Result: **one new direct candidate, clinically valuable for the noncardiac-dominant physiology route.**

### Wave G — multilingual searches

Modern terminology searches were repeated in Portuguese, Spanish, French, Italian and German variants.

Result: **no new individually identifiable direct case.**

### Wave H — recent/grey-literature sweep

Recent and conference signals were sought through 2026. The relevant new records had already been captured by the Cantrell and hidden-series waves (2024 abstract, Ortiz 2025, Zhu 2025/2026).

Result: **no additional direct patient beyond already captured records/signals.**

### Wave I — backward citation chasing

Backward references from direct candidates, especially the historical and modern sentinel reports, were chased for earlier Morgagni/subcostosternal + TOF reports.

Result: **no earlier individually verified direct patient beyond Johnson 1952; references largely looped into background Morgagni literature or non-Morgagni TOF–CDH cases.**

### Wave J — forward citation chasing

Forward/related-citation searches on the modern direct reports and historical candidate were reviewed for later cases.

Result: **no additional individually extractable direct patient after reconciliation.**

### Wave K — final exact/semantic plateau checks

Additional exact-phrase, series, adult, Cantrell, historical and association searches were performed after the last positive hidden-series discoveries.

Result: **no new individually extractable direct patient.**

## 4. Plateau assessment

The late search trajectory is now stable:

- historical expansion produced Johnson 1952;
- hidden-series mining produced Sönmez 2006 and the Ortiz two-patient aggregate signal;
- adult searching produced Rao 2014;
- subsequent multilingual, recent/grey, backward-citation, forward-citation, large-series and final exact/semantic waves did **not** produce another individually extractable direct patient.

The requirement for multiple conceptually independent zero-yield late waves is therefore met.

## 5. Saturation decision

**SCIENTIFIC DISCOVERY / CITATION SATURATION: PASS.**

The current raw literature landscape is sufficiently saturated to stop active discovery expansion and prepare for normalization/deduplication once the formal Phase 1 reproducibility requirement is resolved.

This decision does **not** mean that no unindexed or inaccessible case could exist. It means that continued searching across the pre-specified and subsequently justified sensitivity pathways has reached a defensible diminishing-return plateau.

## 6. Current direct signal at saturation

The saturation registry contains:

- **8 individually identifiable direct candidate records/patient-level reports** with explicit or historically equivalent TOF + Morgagni signal, pending formal Phase 2 identity reconciliation and Phase 5 eligibility;
- **2 additional TOF-associated Morgagni patients reported only at aggregate level** in the Ortiz 2025 series, not currently individually characterizable;
- additional aggregate/unresolved cardiac-series signals that must not be converted into direct patient counts without primary-source evidence;
- one high-value TOF + large CDH management analog with Morgagni anatomy still unresolved;
- explicit non-Morgagni negative/anatomic controls.

Publication count is not yet patient count. No final unique-case number is frozen at Phase 1.

## 7. Important scientific conclusion from the search landscape

The association itself is clearly precedented and rare enough to remain educationally valuable.

The literature already demonstrates materially different routes through the combined problem, including:

- cardiac palliation/repair before hernia recognition;
- hernia-first recognition/repair;
- direct or combined surgical pathways;
- conservative management in a repaired-TOF adult when other physiology dominated;
- a modern RVOT-stent bridge before combined definitive repair in an anatomy-pending TOF + CDH case.

TOF balloon pulmonary/RVOT palliation is well established in the broader TOF literature. The Phase 1 searches did **not identify a direct TOF–Morgagni precedent** for the narrower index-case pattern of a planned RVOT stent being deliberately omitted after satisfactory response to balloon dilation alone. This is a **search finding, not a final novelty claim**, and remains subject to full-text extraction and Phase 10 adjudication.
