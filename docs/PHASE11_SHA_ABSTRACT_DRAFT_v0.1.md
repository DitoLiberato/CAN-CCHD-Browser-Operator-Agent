## Proposed title

**Clinically Actionable Non-CCHD Diagnoses After Failed Newborn Pulse Oximetry Screening: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis**

## Abstract v0.1

**Introduction:** Pulse oximetry screening for critical congenital heart disease (CCHD) generates failed screens in newborns who ultimately do not have target CCHD. Whether these “false-positive” screens represent clinically useful detection of other neonatal disease remains incompletely quantified.

**Methodology:** We performed a systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of newborn pulse-oximetry screening studies reporting outcomes after final failed screens. PubMed, Europe PMC, LILACS/BVS, SciELO and regional/public sources were searched, with citation chasing and overlap adjudication. The primary denominator was harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens. Strict clinically actionable non-CCHD (CAN-CCHD) required documented treatment, escalation, altered disposition or clinically required follow-up; Expanded CAN-CCHD additionally included clinically relevant diagnoses without documented qualifying actionability. A one-stage binomial-logistic-normal GLMM with exact binomial likelihood was prespecified.

**Results:** Twenty-eight independent primary units contributed 1,999 harmonized-CCHD-negative failed screens. The Strict median-study probability was 17.0% (95% CI 3.1–46.8%), with marginal mean 33.8%, tau=3.369 and a 95% prediction interval of 0.03–99.34%, indicating extreme heterogeneity. Expanded CAN-CCHD was 69.4% (57.7–81.4%), with marginal mean 65.8%, tau=1.110 and prediction interval 20.4–95.2%. Outcome-specific pooled probabilities were 10.3% for persistent pulmonary hypertension/pulmonary hypertension, 8.7% for respiratory disease, 16.7% for infection/sepsis and 26.6% for other non-target structural cardiac diagnoses. Sensitivity analyses did not materially change the conclusions. Screening timing did not show a clear subgroup effect.

**Conclusion:** Failed newborn CCHD pulse-oximetry screens frequently identify clinically relevant disease even when target CCHD is absent. The proportion with specifically documented actionable consequences varies markedly across screening programs, but the broader burden of alternative clinically relevant disease is consistently high.

## Scientific positioning notes

1. The abstract deliberately avoids calling 17.0% a universal patient-level prevalence. It identifies it as the median-study probability and immediately supplies heterogeneity context.
2. The Expanded result carries the clearest clinical message and is therefore presented beside, not beneath, the Strict endpoint.
3. Etiologic outcomes are reported separately and are not implied to be mutually exclusive.
4. Timing is described only as showing no clear subgroup effect; no equivalence claim is made.
5. No publication-bias/funnel claim is included.
6. The current title is the protocol-suggested title and is comfortably within the SHA 40-word limit. A more attention-grabbing alternative can be considered later without changing the scientific content.
