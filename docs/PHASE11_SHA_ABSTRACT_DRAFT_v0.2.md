## Title decision for v0.2

### Recommended candidate

**Beyond CCHD: Clinically Relevant Disease After Failed Newborn Pulse Oximetry Screening — A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis**

Rationale: this keeps the focus on pediatric cardiology/CCHD, avoids making `actionable` the title-level claim when Strict is extremely heterogeneous, and communicates the more stable broader clinical finding without implying that every Expanded diagnosis had a documented management consequence.

## Abstract v0.2

**Introduction:** Newborn pulse oximetry is designed to detect critical congenital heart disease (CCHD), yet many infants with final failed screens do not have target CCHD and are conventionally classified as “false positives.” We quantified clinically relevant alternative disease and documented management consequences among these infants.

**Methodology:** We systematically reviewed newborn CCHD pulse-oximetry screening studies reporting outcomes after final failed screens. The primary denominator was harmonized-CCHD-negative final failed screens. Strict clinically actionable non-CCHD (CAN-CCHD) required documented treatment, escalation, altered disposition, or clinically required follow-up; Expanded CAN-CCHD additionally included clinically relevant diagnoses without documented qualifying actionability. Random-effects proportions were synthesized using a one-stage exact-binomial logistic-normal model.

**Results:** Twenty-eight independent primary units contributed 1,999 harmonized-CCHD-negative failed screens. Strict CAN-CCHD had a median-study probability of 17.0% (95% CI 3.1–46.8%) and marginal mean of 33.8%; between-study heterogeneity was extreme (tau=3.369). Expanded CAN-CCHD was substantially more common and less heterogeneous: median-study probability 69.4% (57.7–81.4%), marginal mean 65.8%, tau=1.110. Among outcome-specific reporting subsets, median-study probabilities were 26.6% for other/non-target structural cardiac diagnoses, 16.7% for infection/sepsis, 10.3% for persistent pulmonary hypertension/pulmonary hypertension, and 8.7% for respiratory disease. Sensitivity analyses did not reverse the interpretation.

**Conclusion:** Among final failed CCHD screens without target CCHD, clinically relevant alternative disease was common, whereas specifically documented management consequences varied markedly across programs. These findings challenge the assumption that non-CCHD failed screens are clinically uninformative “false positives” and support evaluating pulse-oximetry screening value beyond target-CCHD detection alone.

## Editorial assessment

The v0.2 candidate is stronger than v0.1 for a SHA conference audience because it:

1. states the clinical problem before the statistical construct;
2. preserves Strict as the prespecified actionability endpoint without making its heterogeneous 17.0% estimate the headline claim;
3. foregrounds the more stable Expanded burden after establishing the Strict hierarchy;
4. keeps the four clinically recognizable etiologic signals, strengthening cardiology/pediatric relevance;
5. removes the weakly useful timing sentence and detailed prediction intervals from the 300-word abstract;
6. converts the conclusion into a clinically meaningful but defensible reframing of the term `false positive`.
